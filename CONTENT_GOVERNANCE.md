# Content Governance

The library is a cultural knowledge service, not an authority-generating machine.

## Provenance
Content should identify its source, attribution, editorial status, and relevant dates where known. Missing provenance must remain missing; it must not be filled with plausible invented facts.

## Cultural claims
Historical, political, linguistic, and cultural claims require an identifiable source or an explicit editorial/contemporary status. Fixture content is demonstration data and must never silently masquerade as authoritative cultural record.

## Editorial layers
The API must preserve distinctions among archival/source material, editorial framing, interpretation, contemporary contribution, and speculation. Consumers may render these differently.

## Publishing
A resource is not considered publishable merely because it validates structurally. Publication status is a separate lifecycle concern.

## Corrections
Content corrections should preserve traceability where the source/provenance model supports it. Do not silently rewrite historical claims to make UI copy convenient.

## Baraza
Baraza content must distinguish historical philosophical material from modern interpretation and open inquiry. The API must not encode speculative synthesis as historical fact.
